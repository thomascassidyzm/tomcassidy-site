### 1.4 The no-civil-war doctrine

Alexander first, because Tom introduced the doctrine off the back of him.

**The doctrine.**

> "OK, so the notion of the everydayness, one thing that's important, Is to not make it a problem to
> be in the script. And to not make it a problem to be putting in the stuff that... The script is
> putting in for you. I mean, it's just noticing that is the genius. That's the separation."

> "Because what tends to happen There's a lot of time people know what they should be doing, but
> they're not doing it. So they've got this disconnect, this separation, which becomes an internal
> civil war between the devil and the angel on their shoulder. Well, you know that you should be
> doing this. You know you should be eating better, but you're eating cake, you like cake. You know
> you should be exercising more, but you're kind of a bit tired. You know you should be reaching out
> to more people, but you're kind of worried about rejection."

> "So it's this internal civil war between knowing what you should be doing and It uses up all their
> energy. In my experience of working over the last 30 years with people is that If they can avoid
> the internal civil war, then their energy is free to do all the good stuff."

> "And so what you're doing with people is all you're doing is getting them to notice. What they are
> choosing, because really, If it is something that they would choose, then it's authentically them.
> But if it's something that they would not choose, then it's the script. And leave it there. Just
> notice that. Oh, that's my script. And not, oh, I must do better. No, that's just the script."

**The script deserves thanks, not war.**

> "It's completely natural, and it's been there for years and years and years. Most people who want
> to affect some kind of change in their lives are probably over the age of 20, which means the
> script has had something like 20 years of practice on them. And, you know, the script isn't a
> terrible thing in itself. It did stop us probably wanting to jump off a cliff just for the hell of
> it, or eat anything that we weren't sure what it was. Or, you know, maybe befriend somebody who
> might perhaps want to stab us. So it was designed. To avoid us being eaten or challenged or to deal
> with any kind of unusual and uncertain situation."

> "But the reality these days is most of the certainty that we want In life is an illusion and these
> days there's very few things that are actually going to eat us. Most of the things that are going
> to be problematic for us are the script itself which will prevent us from trying, prevent us from
> really doing things because it'll want to keep us safe. So that's a little bit more on that."

**[ASSEMBLER]** The formal statement: *would-choose = authentically you; would-not-choose = script;
the diagnostic is the gap, the practice is noticing the gap without judgment, and that's all.*
Forgiveness here is not kindness but thermodynamics — any design element that induces guilt
(streaks, misses, "do better") re-ignites the civil war and burns the very fuel it is meant to
create. Champion versus brother-in-law is the archetypal polarity of the whole system.

## 3. Contradictions and drift — the editing queue

Stated plainly, not softened. Each one is a ruling for Tom; none is resolved here.

**3.1 Dogma 3 means two different things.** Spoken, discernment is *selection*: read the three most
popular books, extract what works for people who already have what you want, then choose which of it
fits your starting point. Written, `third-dogma-discernment.md` never mentions books, experts or
personal fit — it is a full essay on the epistemology of evidence: correlation versus causation,
sample size, "research is in the past", and a checklist of questions to ask of any claimed finding.
Both are real, both are Tom's, and they are different operations wearing one name. **[ASSEMBLER]**
My read: the spoken version is the *dogma* — the step you actually perform — and the written essay is
the *skill* the dogma requires. If that is right, the essay is missing its opening half rather than
being wrong. That is the single biggest structural gap in the written canon, and it takes one
sentence from Tom to settle which way round it goes.

**3.2 "The 13th one is usually the review one" holds in three of eight shipped programmes.** Review
at week 13: Reasonable Leadership, Conquering Life, Agile Results. Not review at week 13: Reasonable
Eating (Algorithm Zero, EAT MORE, always-on and never in rotation), Reasonable Success ("It's an
inside job", always-on), Great Teaching (the G that completes GREAT TEACHING), the Ultimate 13×4
(NOTICE, at week **1**, "where every lap begins"), Reasonable Teaching (Self-Belief keystone at week
1, and only nine weeks). So the estate carries three distinct hub roles — *always-on Algorithm Zero*,
*week-1 opener*, *week-13 review* — and no written rule says when to use which. The flagship
programme uses the one Tom's spoken rule does not describe.

**3.3 Reasonable Eating's domains: Tom said four and named three.** Spoken: "we had four domains. When
to eat, why to eat, and then what to eat." Shipped: HOW / WHEN / WHY / WHAT, three focus points each,
`reasonable-eating.ts:4`. The count is right; HOW went missing in the telling — and HOW is the domain
that carries week 1, "Chew until liquid", the very focus he used as his own example of what the
reminders say. Recorded because a canon built from the transcript alone would ship a three-domain
Reasonable Eating.

**3.4 Alexander as coach contradicts the estate's own boundary.** Tom, spoken: "the individual
coaching, kind of the, really the Tom in your pocket, and we might keep it as Alexander". But
`SCRIPT-CANON.md` §4 states the opposite as a settled finding — "The coaching persona is Tom himself,
not Alexander", citing the Thrive engine's literal instruction "You are Tom" — and draws the rule of
thumb that if the move ends in *understanding*, it is Alexander, and if it ends in *choosing*, it is
the Script method in Tom's voice. `the-format.md` reinforces it: "He's the same Alexander everywhere
I use him — he isn't reinvented for each site." Tom's proposal would make Alexander both. This is a
genuine fork, and it is his to rule on. **[ASSEMBLER]** His own telling already contains a clean
split that would resolve it — Alexander-as-Socratic-teacher for the setup, someone else as
champion-in-your-pocket for the daily companion — but he floated using the one name for both.

**3.5 The civil war: avoid it, or fight it?** Spoken doctrine is unambiguous — the internal civil war
is the thing that burns the energy, and the whole practice is designed so it never starts. But
`reasonable-doubt.md:242` closes its central section with: "all of life is a daily dance between
these two opposing positions, **a continuous battle in the fight to discern the difference**." That
is the war reframed as the practice. The two are not obviously reconcilable in the same document, and
the essay's framing is the one currently published.

**3.6 The name: Reason-Ability, or The CASSIDY Method?** Turn 5 is a defence of "Reason-Ability" as
the right name. But `fourth-dogma-a-reasonable-plan.md:50` records it as already superseded: "In fact

Method made it somehow more eponymous…" — and `the-cassidy-method.md` ships as a standalone essay
presenting the Method as the container, with the 13/4 as one of its two flavours. The spoken take
