## 1. The zero-setup invariant

> "Things have to be done without any kind of requirements for equipment, even clothing.
> They have to be done in a lift. In a hotel room. In a garden, in any scenario."

## 2. Willpower = planning + setup time — his definition of minimum will for movement

> "These have to be things that can be done with a minimum amount of willpower and…
> usually a minimum amount of willpower translates into *I can do this now*. So, as soon
> as I think of it, I can do it. Willpower correlates really with the planning required,
> the setup time."

The gym as the canonical failure:

> "If it's going to the gym, well, you've got to be part of a gym, you've got a plan to get
> there, you've got to get your gear on. You've got to make sure the gear is clean, you've
> got to have a membership… you've got to worry about parking. All those things. That's not
> a minimum will system. It's not a minimum will system by my definition."

**[ASSEMBLER]** This is a measurable definition, not a vibe: will-cost ≈ setup latency.
The test of any candidate focus point is "can it start within seconds of being thought of?"

## 3. The combined-exercise principle, and the press-up as front runner

> "The exercise needs to use as many different… You can't have lots of different exercises.
> It has to be a combined exercise."

> "One of them is the humble press-up. And the press-up is a minimum-will exercise thing.
> It can be used going, you know, even starting on your knees. And it can be used to going
> full body and complete sort of down into the corner with your chest sort of rotated and
> sort of shoulder right into the corner, face right into each corner. So you've got a lot
> of body stuff going on as well… of the last few years of my experiments, the press-up is
> the clear front runner."

**[ASSEMBLER]** Note the press-up as described is itself a ladder — knees → full body →
corner variations with rotation — i.e. it carries its own coarse-to-fine progression, which
is the same grammar as every other Reason-Ability artefact.

## 4. Always be stretching

> "Stretching is another one, doing lots of stretching. So always be stretching, making
> sure that kind of the flexibility and that sort of tendency to be able to make sure you
> can move about your own axis and do all those sorts of things competently, so stretching's
> got to be a big part of it, and I guess that extends into things like yoga and so on."

## 5. No weights, no strength training

> "There can be no weights involved, there can be no strength training involved."

**[ASSEMBLER]** This rules the movement scout's flagged "strength gap" question: the gap is
not accepted-as-stated, it is *dissolved by design* — bodyweight (the press-up ladder) is the
strength story, and equipment-based strength training fails principle 1 and principle 2.

## 6. The options heuristic — choose the moving option

> "Whenever there are options, you decide to take the one which is going to have the best
> long-term payout, which will have the best total movement use. So, for example, if the
> option is to go up the stairs or to get a lift, you always go up the stairs… When you're
> going on the underground, always walk up the escalator, don't just stand on it… If you
> can walk, you don't need to run. This is a minimum will system. Cycle — get a bike
> without an electric component so you're always having to work."

The apartment story, with his own honesty about its scope:

> "I famously [chose] to rent an apartment on the… floor of the highest building that
> doesn't have a lift, which means that basically when you get to the end of the day,
> you've got really only two options. You either sleep on the street or you walk up the
> six flights of stairs. So it's building a system that's… not a minimum will system
> because not everyone is in a position where they can do that. But when there are options,
> make the decision to choose the option which is going to give you the better long-term
> payoff."

## 7. His own structural sorting

> "They're kind of like heuristics. They're not going to be focus points so much as a more
> general thing which is: choose wisely whenever you're presented with options."

**[ASSEMBLER]** Tom pre-sorted his own material into the programme's grammar without being
asked: the options heuristic is HUB-SHAPED (always-on, every day, exactly the role EAT MORE
plays for eating), while the press-up ladder and stretching are FOCUS-POINT-SHAPED (weekly
attention). Two further observations for the selection sitting, offered not decided:
(a) the options heuristic is the possibility-space ethics applied to movement — choose the
branch with the best trajectory-level payout — so Shape's MOVE hub would be the CE mechanism
in pedestrian clothing; (b) the apartment story is *environment design*: a selection frozen
into the world so the script never gets a vote — beyond minimum-will (as he says himself),
but the natural "extremely nuanced" top of the same ladder.
